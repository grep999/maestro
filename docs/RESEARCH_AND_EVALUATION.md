# Research and Evaluation Principles

Maestro is domain-agnostic; research methods must be selected to fit the question, not imposed as one universal methodology.

## Ground the question
State the decision the research must inform, intended audience, scope, time horizon, constraints and what would change the decision. Separate descriptive, causal, predictive and normative questions.

## Source hierarchy
Prefer primary records, official documentation, original datasets and direct observations. Use high-quality secondary synthesis to contextualize. Treat commentary and community reports as leads unless independently corroborated. Record source identity, publication/update date, retrieval date, relevant passage or data, limitations and conflicts of interest.

## Evidence ledger
For each material claim record:
- claim and confidence;
- source and exact locator;
- whether the source directly supports the claim;
- corroboration and contradictions;
- freshness and scope limits;
- what evidence would falsify or materially change the claim.

Do not convert absence of evidence into evidence of absence without justification.

## Evaluation
Define criteria before judging options. Consider effectiveness, feasibility, cost, time, risk, reversibility, maintainability, ethical and legal constraints, and unintended consequences as relevant to the domain. Distinguish observed outcomes from forecasts and assumptions.

## Decision record
Capture the decision, alternatives considered, evidence, uncertainty, rationale, owner, date, review trigger and conditions for reversal. Keep the record concise enough to remain usable.

## Learning
Compare forecasts with outcomes. Update confidence and assumptions when evidence changes. Preserve negative results and failed hypotheses so the workflow does not repeat avoidable mistakes.
